chunky.fm LIVE

One sheet, one accent, and one thing allowed to be bright.

The station is a near-black sheet with translucent panels floating on it. White is the only accent and it means you can press this. Red is the only signal and it means the station is on the air right now. Every other value in the system is a grey chosen so that those two stay the loudest things on the screen.

Every value below is read out of the station's own stylesheet as this page loads, so it cannot describe a design the station has stopped using.

The rule

Three sentences that decide almost everything else

If a question is not answered further down, it is answered here. These are the reasons the palette is shaped the way it is, so something new can be judged against them rather than against a list of swatches.

Brightness is a budget

On a page where a single red dot means the station is on the air, a second bright thing is noise. Give a layout exactly one loud element and let the greys hold everything else. If two things are competing, one of them is wrong.

Red is reserved

Red never decorates. It appears on the LIVE badge, on the level meter's mark, on a mic break and on the control that ends a broadcast. Anywhere else it is a lie about the station's state. Where a red surface is genuinely needed it is the wash, never the solid fill.

Nothing moves without something to report

The meter goes flat over silence. The record stops rather than gliding to a stylish halt. A badge that goes off air keeps its exact shape and loses its colour. Motion here is a readout, and where there is nothing to read out it holds still.

Colour

Eleven values, and only two of them are colours

Click a swatch to copy its hex. The grey line under each is the binding part: the role is the rule, and the hex is only how the role is spelled this week. Two of these are translucent by design, so their chips are shown over the sheet, which is the only place they ever sit.

Surfaces darkest to lightest

Text brightest to quietest

Signals state only, never decoration

For anything that is not a browser. Translucency means nothing in an email client or a printer. Each swatch above carries its flattened equivalent, composited over the sheet, and that is the value to type into a tool that has no idea what is behind it. The two hairlines flatten the same way, and their values are in Shape.

Contrast

What each colour is actually cleared to carry

Measured against the sheet as the page loads. One value in the set does not clear the threshold for normal body text, and it is worth knowing which before it ends up carrying a paragraph somebody has to read.

Colour Token On the sheet Cleared for

The one to watch is --faint. It sits just under the 4.5:1 that normal text needs. That is correct for the small uppercase labels it was drawn for, where a brighter line always sits beside it and the label is not the thing being read. It is wrong for a paragraph, for an email footer, and for an unsubscribe link. Those move up to --muted.

Type

Space Grotesk speaks, Cousine counts

Two faces and one division of labour. Space Grotesk carries everything said in words. Cousine carries everything that is a number, a code or a machine label, so a digit changing never shifts what sits beside it. Both are under the SIL Open Font License and both are vendored into the bundle, which is what lets the station serve its own design with no outbound network at all.

This table is the one thing on the page nobody can read off a token. The scale lives in the rules of styles.css and landing/landing.css rather than in a variable, so these numbers are remembered rather than measured. If the scale changes, this is wrong until somebody changes it too.

Negative tracking above, positive below. Everything from panel-title size upward tightens as it grows, which is most of what keeps a large heading reading as one object rather than a row of words. Only the small uppercase labels open up, and only in Cousine. Never letterspace lowercase Space Grotesk at body size.

Fallback stacks, which matter more off the web than on it. Webfonts do not load in Outlook for Windows and are unreliable in several mobile clients, so always write the whole stack: 'Space Grotesk', 'Helvetica Neue', Arial, sans-serif and 'Cousine', 'Courier New', monospace. Cousine is metric-compatible with Courier New, so the numeric face degrades without moving a layout. Space Grotesk to Arial does move, so leave a headline box room to grow.

Shape

The radius says what kind of thing it is

Radius here is a category rather than a taste. A full pill means you can press it, or it is reporting a state. A 16px corner means it is a container. The turntable's 28px belongs to the turntable.

Edges are rings, not borders. Every hairline in the system is drawn as an inset 1px shadow, at where it is only describing an edge and where the edge has to carry weight. That is deliberate rather than clever: a panel keeps its exact size whether it has a ring or not, so nothing shifts by a pixel when a card gains an edge on hover. In any medium that cannot do an inset shadow, substitute a real 1px border at or .

Drop shadows are rare, and there are two. The turntable carries 0 25px 50px rgb(0 0 0 / 25%) and the floating bar carries a softer version of the same. A card on the sheet gets a ring and nothing else. If two blocks need a shadow to separate them, what is actually wrong is the space between them.

Objects

The five things that make it recognisable

These are drawn by shared.css, which the station and the landing page both import, which is why they are identical in both. The specimens below are those same rules rather than copies of them, so what is on this page is what is on the station. Anything new should be assembled out of them before it is drawn from scratch.

The wordmark

chunky.fm chunky.fm

Always lowercase, always the word and the suffix with no space between them. Bold, tracked in by a hair. The name takes --fg and the .fm takes --faint, and that grey is the whole lockup: there is no symbol beside it, no stacked variant and no version in capitals. Below about 14px the suffix stops separating, so that is the floor.

The LIVE badge

LIVE OFF AIR

The loudest object in the identity, and the reason nothing else is allowed to be red. Off the air it keeps its exact footprint and loses its colour, so the space it occupies never moves when the station stops. Do not resize it, do not restate it as a solid red fill, and do not put it on anything that is not about a broadcast happening now.

The buttons

One white pill for the thing the group is for, and quiet grey for everything standing beside it. Two heights and one rule for choosing: the larger where the control is the point of whatever it sits in, the smaller where it stands in a row of its peers. The destructive one is a wash rather than a fill, because a solid red button would be a second bright thing competing with the badge.

The deck

340px square with a 300px well inside it, and fixed rather than fluid: a record that changed size with the window would stop reading as an object sitting on a page. The sheen and the spindle hold still while the record turns, because a spindle is part of the deck rather than part of the record. It turns at a speed you can read as turning rather than at 33⅓, and it stops when the audio stops. The tonearm rides over all of this and is drawn in Turntable.tsx; it is left off here rather than copied, since a copy is a thing that drifts.

The level meter

LVL

3px white bars, 3px apart, in a 32px band. Over silence every bar sits flat rather than carrying on dancing, and that is the one place this whole system is most easily made to lie: a meter moving over nothing is the only element here capable of actively misreporting the station. In a still piece such as an email, draw it flat, and never animate it as decoration.

Space

One inner padding, and gaps large enough to look like mistakes

Spacing runs on a four-pixel grid. Two numbers do most of the work, and one rule about section rhythm explains why the landing page's gaps look wrong in a screenshot and correct on a screen.

Why the gaps are that large. The station is a room you sit in, where everything has to be visible at once. A page in front of it is something you are read to, at the pace of somebody scrolling, and its sections are spaced so you rarely see two of them whole. One idea per screen. Carry that into an email: one message per block, and more air between blocks than feels comfortable in the layout tool.

What changes on the way out of a browser. Body copy goes up a step, because the web's 14px is read on a screen somebody chose to look at and an email is read wherever it lands. Every translucent fill becomes the flattened value from Colour. Every inset ring becomes a real 1px border. Everything else stays exactly as it is.

The pill will not round in Outlook for Windows. Word's rendering engine ignores border-radius. Either accept a square button there, which still reads correctly as white on the sheet, or wrap it in a VML roundrect fallback. Do not solve it with a picture of a button: images are blocked by default in several clients, and a blocked image on a dark template leaves a white hole where the one thing you wanted pressed used to be.

Dark templates meet clients that force light. Ship the wordmark as live text rather than as an image, so it can inherit a colour instead of becoming a white rectangle. Give the badge a real flattened fill rather than letting it rely on the body behind it. Set background-color on the outer table as well as on the body. Single column, 600px, alt text on everything.

Do & don't

The mistakes this system invites

Every one of these looks reasonable on its own and breaks the identity in place.

Do

  • Give a layout one bright element and let grey carry the rest.
  • Reach for an object that already exists before drawing a new one.
  • Set times, counts and codes in Cousine so columns hold still.
  • Use the red wash where a red surface is genuinely needed.
  • Leave more air between blocks than the layout tool suggests.
  • Keep the badge's footprint identical on and off the air.
  • Move footers and unsubscribe links up to --muted.

Don't

  • Fill a button with solid --live.
  • Use red for emphasis, for a price, or for a heading.
  • Add a sixth grey, a gradient accent, or a second signal colour.
  • Set the wordmark in capitals, space the suffix off, or lock it up with a symbol.
  • Animate the meter or the record where there is no audio to report.
  • Set a paragraph in --faint.
  • Add a drop shadow to separate two blocks that only need space.
  • Letterspace lowercase Space Grotesk at body size.

The colours, the ratios and the sizes on this page are read out of src/tokens.css as it loads, and the objects are drawn by src/shared.css. The type scale and the section rhythm are the only figures typed in by hand, because they live in rules rather than in variables. If anything here disagrees with the stylesheets, the stylesheets are right.
