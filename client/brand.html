chunky.fm LIVE

Ink, newsprint, and two colours of riso.

The station is printed as a poster. Ink and newsprint in solid bands, edge to edge, with square corners. Riso blue is the only colour that means this starts sound. Fluoro pink marks a count or an index and is never an action. Red still means one thing: the station is on the air right now. Everything else is paper.

Every value below is read out of the station's own stylesheet as this page loads, so it cannot describe a design the station has stopped using. The source is the Figma page "revamp — poster" and its "poster" variables.

The rule

Three sentences that decide almost everything else

If a question is not answered further down, it is answered here. These are the reasons the palette is shaped the way it is, so something new can be judged against them rather than against a list of swatches.

Printed, not lit

Solid bands of ink and paper, edge to edge. No glass, no blur, no glow and no drop shadow. If two blocks need separating, use a 1px rule or a change of band. A poster has no rounded corners, so nothing here has one unless it is round in life: a record, a dot.

Each ink has one job

Press blue starts sound: Tune in, Play. Mark pink is a number or an index: a queue position, a tally, every third wish. Red is on the air and nothing else. A colour used outside its job is a lie about what the thing does.

Nothing moves without something to report

The meter goes flat over silence. The record stops rather than gliding to a stylish halt. A badge that goes off air keeps its exact shape and loses its colour. Motion here is a readout, and where there is nothing to read out it holds still. The ticker is the one exception, and it stops on hover.

Contrast

What each colour is actually cleared to carry

Measured as the page loads, on ink and on newsprint, because the poster prints on both. Most of these clear the threshold on one ground and fail on the other, and that is worth knowing before a colour ends up on the wrong band.

Colour Token On ink On newsprint Cleared for

Smudge has two spellings for this reason. --smudge is quiet type on paper and reads as a hole on ink, so quiet type on a dark band takes --smudge-on-ink instead. Press is a fill, not a type colour. It carries newsprint type on top of it and is not cleared to be type itself on either ground.

Type

Big Shoulders shouts, Cousine says everything else

Three faces, and a strict division of labour. Big Shoulders Display is for headlines and nothing else. Cousine sets every label, button, time and name in capitals and every sentence in sentence case. Space Grotesk is kept for one job only: text somebody reads at length while music plays, which is the chat and the lyrics. All three are under the SIL Open Font License and vendored into the bundle.

Capitals are tracked out, sentences are not. Every label and button is Cousine in capitals at +0.08em. A headline is Big Shoulders in capitals, set tight. Never set a sentence in the display face, and never letterspace a sentence.

Fallback stacks, which matter more off the web than on it. Webfonts do not load in Outlook for Windows and are unreliable in several mobile clients, so always write the whole stack: 'Big Shoulders Display', 'Arial Narrow', Impact, sans-serif and 'Cousine', 'Courier New', monospace. Cousine is metric-compatible with Courier New, so the body degrades without moving a layout. Arial Narrow is wider than Big Shoulders, so leave a headline box room to grow.

Shape

Square, with two exceptions

A poster has no rounded corners, so every box, button, field and badge is square. --poster-radius is a name for zero rather than a value anybody should be tempted to change. Two shapes are not rectangles, and each belongs to one object.

Edges are rules. Every hairline is 1px, drawn as an inset shadow at where it only describes an edge and where it has to carry weight, so a panel keeps its exact size whether it has one or not. On paper the rule is --rule-light. In any medium that cannot do an inset shadow, substitute a real 1px border at or .

There are no drop shadows. Not on the deck, not on the bar, not on a card. A printed page has no light source. If two blocks need a shadow to separate them, what is actually wrong is the band or the space between them.

Objects

The six things that make it recognisable

These are drawn by shared.css and poster/poster.css, which the station imports, which is why they are identical in both. The specimens below are those same rules rather than copies of them, so what is on this page is what is on the station. Anything new should be assembled out of them before it is drawn from scratch.

The wordmark

chunky.fm chunky.fm

Big Shoulders Display at 900, in capitals, with no space between the word and the suffix. The name takes --fg and the .fm takes --faint, and that is the whole lockup: there is no symbol beside it and no stacked variant. At the foot of a page it is set huge, edge to edge, like the name across the bottom of a poster.

The LIVE tab

LIVE OFF AIR

Solid red, square, ink type in monospace capitals. It is the one place red is printed solid, and the reason nothing else is allowed to be red. Off the air it keeps its exact footprint, loses its fill and keeps a rule, so the space it takes never moves when the station stops. Do not put it on anything that is not about a broadcast happening now.

The buttons

Square, Cousine 700 in capitals at +0.08em. Press blue only where the button starts sound. A plain button is newsprint on ink, for the thing the group is for, and the quiet one is an outline beside it. Two heights: the larger where the control is the point of whatever it sits in. The destructive one is a red wash with a red rule, because a solid red button would be read as LIVE.

The picture slot

MJ-05 · Host portrait Placeholder

Every picture that does not exist yet is a slot: bone paper, a thin cross from corner to corner, and the asset's id in a tab. The id is the same one in Figma and in docs/midjourney-prompts.md, so a picture has one name from the prompt to the pixel. slot--dark is the same box printed on charcoal, for a slot inside a dark band. Drop the file into src/assets/poster/ and set it in poster/assets.ts; the box already has the picture's size, so nothing moves.

The deck

340px square with a 300px well inside it, printed flat: a charcoal plate with a rule round it, no gradient and no shadow. The record and its label are the only round things on the station. The sleeve on the label is printed grey and hard, the way the landing page prints sleeves. It turns at a speed you can read as turning, and it stops when the audio stops. The tonearm rides over all of this and is drawn in Turntable.tsx; it is left off here rather than copied, since a copy is a thing that drifts.

The level meter

LVL

3px newsprint bars, square-ended, 3px apart, in a 32px band. Over silence every bar sits flat rather than carrying on dancing: a meter moving over nothing is the one element here capable of actively misreporting the station. In a still piece such as an email, draw it flat, and never animate it as decoration.

Space

One gutter, and bands that run edge to edge

Spacing runs on a four-pixel grid. A poster is built from full-width bands, so the measures that matter are the gutter every band shares and the height of a band, not the width of a centred column.

Why the bands are that tall. The station is a room you sit in, where everything has to be visible at once. A page in front of it is something you are read to, at the pace of somebody scrolling, one band per idea. Carry that into an email: one message per block, and a change of band rather than a box to separate them.

What changes on the way out of a browser. Body copy goes up a step, because an email is read wherever it lands. Every inset rule becomes a real 1px border. The display face will not load in most clients, so the headline falls back to Arial Narrow or Impact; write the stack in full.

Square corners are the easy part. Outlook for Windows ignores border-radius, which used to be a problem for the white pill and is not one for a square press-blue button. Do not replace the button with a picture of a button: images are blocked by default in several clients.

Dark templates meet clients that force light. Ship the wordmark as live text rather than as an image, so it can inherit a colour instead of becoming a white rectangle. Give the LIVE tab a real fill. Set background-color on the outer table as well as on the body. Single column, 600px, alt text on everything.

Do & don't

The mistakes this system invites

Every one of these looks reasonable on its own and breaks the identity in place.

Do

  • Build a page from solid bands, edge to edge.
  • Keep press blue for the one control that starts sound.
  • Set labels, buttons and times in Cousine capitals at +0.08em.
  • Leave a labelled slot where a picture is not ready yet.
  • Print sleeves and portraits grey and hard, like a photocopy.
  • Keep the LIVE tab's footprint identical on and off the air.
  • Use --smudge-on-ink for quiet type on a dark band.

Don't

  • Round a corner, or bring back the white pill.
  • Float a translucent card over a backdrop, or blur anything.
  • Use press blue for a link, or mark pink for an action.
  • Use red for emphasis, for a price, or for a heading.
  • Set a sentence in Big Shoulders Display.
  • Use Space Grotesk anywhere but the chat and the lyrics.
  • Add a drop shadow, a gradient or a glow.
  • Copy Lot Radio's purple and yellow, sticker wall or wordmark.
