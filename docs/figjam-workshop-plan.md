# Ideation workshop — chunky.fm

A plan for the FigJam workshop template with the Quick context, Rapid ideation
and Storyboarding sections. Ninety minutes, run solo or with two or three
people from the interviews.

This is **Step 4 of the case study — Ideate**. It sits after the research and
before any screen design. Its outputs are a screenshot of the sticky wall, two
six-panel storyboards, and one decision about what to design first.

---

## The agenda

| Time | Section | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 – 0:10 | Quick context | Everyone knows the goal and the constraints |
| 0:10 – 0:20 | Reverse brainstorm (the other board) | The sabotage list, already filled |
| 0:20 – 0:35 | Rapid ideation, round 1 | 20+ stickies against the first prompt |
| 0:35 – 0:45 | Rapid ideation, round 2 | 15+ stickies against the second prompt |
| 0:45 – 0:55 | Cluster and vote | Three ideas carried forward |
| 0:55 – 1:20 | Storyboarding | Two six-panel stories |
| 1:20 – 1:30 | Decide | One sentence naming what gets designed first |

Run the two ideation rounds on a visible timer. Rushing is the mechanism — it
is what stops people editing themselves before the sticky lands.

---

## 1. Quick context

Fill the three cards exactly as follows.

### Today's goal

> Decide what the first thirty seconds of chunky.fm should feel like — for the
> person who joins a session already in progress, and for the person who starts
> one.

### Helpful info

Four short lines, so anyone in the room can contribute without reading the
codebase:

- The station plays to about thirty people at once. It is a room, not a
  broadcast.
- Everyone hears the same second of the same record. Joining at 2:14 means
  hearing 2:14.
- There are no accounts. A nickname is the whole sign-up, and it lives on the
  device.
- The station only exists while a host is live. When they stop, it is off air.

### Further reading

Link these three:

- [case-study.md](case-study.md) — the problem, the goal and the constraints
- [research-kit.md](research-kit.md) — the interview findings and the persona
- [figjam-reverse-brainstorm.md](figjam-reverse-brainstorm.md) — the sabotage
  board, which is the warm-up for this session

## 2. Rapid ideation

Two rounds, two prompts, one timer. Write one idea per sticky and do not
discuss anything until the timer stops.

### Round 1 — eight minutes

> **How might we make a person who joins twenty minutes late feel like they
> walked into the room, rather than like they missed it?**

Seed the wall with six stickies so nobody faces a blank canvas, then cover
them up and let people go:

- Show what has already been played tonight, so the evening has a past.
- Say who else arrived late, so being late is normal.
- Name the moment you joined and keep it, like a ticket stub.
- Let the host see someone arrive and greet them by name.
- Fade the newcomer in over a second or two rather than dropping them into the
  middle of a bar.
- Show how far into the record the room is, not how far into it you are.

### Round 2 — six minutes

> **How might we make a person with a record collection actually press "go
> live" for the first time?**

Seed with four:

- Let them play to an empty room first, with nobody watching.
- Show them exactly who can hear, before anything starts.
- Make the first session short by default, so committing is small.
- Let them queue the whole evening in advance, so going live is not
  improvising.

### Clustering and voting

Group by what the sticky is about. Name each cluster in a full sentence. Then
vote: three dots each, spend them all, no explaining before the vote. Carry the
top three clusters into the storyboards.

## 3. Storyboarding

The template gives six panels per participant. Use each strip for one person's
whole experience, not for six screens. Panels 1 and 6 should be life, not
interface — the product only occupies the middle.

### Strip 1 — the listener who arrives late

Rename "Participant 1" to **"The listener — arriving at 21:40"**.

| Panel | What to draw | Caption |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A phone on a kitchen counter, a message notification | "A link lands in the group chat at 21:20. She sees it at 21:40." |
| 2 | A thumb tapping the link, a browser opening | "One tap. No app, no store, no password." |
| 3 | A single field with a cursor in it | "She types a nickname. That is the whole sign-up." |
| 4 | Headphones, a waveform mid-way along | "Music, already in progress. She lands where the room is, not at the start." |
| 5 | A list of names down the side of the screen | "Four people she knows are in here, and the host says her name over the record." |
| 6 | The phone face down, her in the room, still listening | "She puts the phone down. The evening carries on without needing her." |

### Strip 2 — the host going live for the first time

Rename "Participant 2" to **"The host — the first session"**.

| Panel | What to draw | Caption |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Shelves of records, a laptop closed | "He has been making playlists for people for years, and has never played to anyone." |
| 2 | Files dragged onto a browser window | "He drops eight tracks into the page. There is no encoder and no server to set up." |
| 3 | A queue, and a large button | "He builds the running order, then presses go live." |
| 4 | An empty room list with one name appearing | "For ninety seconds nobody is there. Then a name appears, then three more." |
| 5 | A microphone icon, the music dipping behind a voice | "He talks over the intro. The record ducks under him instead of stopping." |
| 6 | The laptop closed, a saved episode | "He ends the session. It becomes an episode anyone can hear tomorrow." |

### Drawing it

Do not draw well. Boxes, stick figures and arrows read as thinking; polished
frames read as a result made afterwards and prove nothing about your process.
Write the caption first and draw to it — a panel that needs three sentences of
caption is two panels.

---

## 4. The decision at the end

Close the session by writing one sentence in a sticky, in the largest text on
the board:

> The first thing I will design is ___, because ___.

Based on the strips above, the honest answer is likely the **join moment** —
panels 3 to 5 of strip 1. It is the only part of the product that no
competitor has, it is where the research pointed, and every other screen can be
conventional without hurting.

---

## 5. What goes in the portfolio

Three images, in this order, with two sentences each:

1. **The sabotage board**, cropped to five or six rows. Caption it with the row
   that changed the design most.
2. **The sticky wall after voting**, with the dots visible. Messy is correct.
3. **Strip 1**, full width. This is the one that tells a stranger what the
   product is, faster than any screenshot of the finished interface.

Keep the raw boards linked rather than embedded. A recruiter wants the three
images; a design lead wants to zoom in, and the link is for them.
